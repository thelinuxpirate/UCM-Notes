** Accounting Assumptions & Principles
*** Assumptions
- Going-Concern Assumption = Assumtion that business will continye instad of being closed or sold
- Monetary Unit Assumption = Express transactions in monetary, or money, units
- Business Entity          = A business is accounted for seperatly from other business entities, including its owner
- Time Period Assumption   = Presumes that the life of a company can be divided into time periods, e.g months & years
*** Principles
- Revenue Recognition Principle
  - Recognize revenue when it is earned
  - Proceeds need not be in cash
  - Measure revenue by cash recived plus cash value of items recived
Golden Rule: When the company provides goods or services record revenue, not just when you get paid

- Cost Principle            = Accounting information is based on actual cost, actual cost is considered objective
- Matching Principle        = A company must record its expenses incurred to generate the revenue recorded
- Full Disclosure Principle = A company is required to report the details behind financial statements that would impcat users' decisions

** Transaction Analysis & The Accounting Equation
The accounting equation:
- Assets = Liabilities + Equity
^ Will always be true

Expanded Equation
                                                            [   Net Income    ] AKA Profit

                       [                      Equity                          ]
*** Accounts
- An Account = Individual asset, liability, equity, revenue, or expense item that a company wants
seperatly identified on its financial records
**** Types
- Assets = Resources owned or controlled by a company | Buildings, Land, Equipment/Supplies (intent to use),
  Accounts(sale from customer, verbal agreement, short-term, no interest)/Notes(loan to customer, written agreement, long-term, interest) Receivable,
  Vehicles, Inventory (intent to sell), Cash

- Liabilites = Creditors' claims on company's assets
  Accounts/Notes payable, Taxes payable, wages payable

- Equity = Owner's claims on assets | Owner, Capital - Owner, Withdrawls + Revenues - Expenses

- Revenues & Expenses = Subset of Equity | increases & decreases of Equity
  AREN'T assets & liabilities, rather they measure the change of assets & liabilites change from operating activites;
  - Revenues = A measure of the inflow of resources form the sale of goods &/or services
  - Expenses = A measure of resources used up in the normal course of operations
*** Transaction Analysis
- The accounting equation MUST remain in balance after each transaction
- At least two accounts are involved in every transaction
- Always look at the transaction from the business' point of view (not owner/employee/customer etc)
Golden Rule: When the company provides goods or services record revenue, not just when you get paid
*Assets = Liabilities + Equity*
** Financial Statements
 - Income Statement | describes a company's revenues & expenses
   along with the resulting net income or loss over a period of time
   due to earnings activities
   - Revenues
   - Expenses
 - Statement of Owner's Equity/Capital | Investments/Withdrawals from the owner
   - Covered through a period of time & net income is required
 - Balance Sheet | describes a company's financial position at a point in time
   - Assets = Liabilities + Equity | Get the total for each, equity's total is measured via L + E
(In Order^)

- The total in a financial statement is shown by two lines under the total

* Chapter 2 | Analyzing & Recording Transacitons
** Analyzing & Recording Process
- Analyze each transaction & event from *source documents*
  - Source Documents: Checks, Bills from Suppliers, Purchase Orders,
    Employee Earnings Records, Bank Statements, Sales Tickets
- Record relevant transactions & events in a *journal*
- Post journal information to *ledger* accounts
  - The General Ledger: Book keeping a record containg all of the accounts used by the company
- Prepare & Analyze the trial balance
** New Accounts
*** Asset Accounts
- Prepaid Accounts (Ex: Subscriptions, Insurance)
*** Liability Accounts
- Accrued Liabilites = Wages Payable, Rent Payable, Interest Payable
- Unearned Revenue   = Customer paid in advance, labor owed
** Owner's Captial Formula (Equity)
- Owner's Investments - Owner's Withdrawals + Revenues - Expenses = Owner's Equity, ending balance
** Debits & Credits
- T-Account (Requires 2 Books) = represents a ledger account & is a tool used to understand the effects of one or more transactions

  *Account Title:*
  
  (Left Side) Debit | (Right Side) Credit
  
- Double-Entry Accounting | An account balance is the difference between the increases & decrases in an account (Ex: T-Account)
- Accounts normally have positive balances; Therefore, asset accounts
  will normally have a net debit balance, liabilities will have a
  net credit balance, etc.
- T-Accounts are useful illustrations, but balance column ledger
  accounts are used in practice
*** Rules
- Assets       = Debit + | Credit -
- Liabilities  = Debit - | Credit +
- Equity       = Debit - | Credit +
- Normal Rules = Debit + | Credit +

- At least 2 accounts are involved with every transaction (DOUBLE-entry accounting)
- The total of all debited accounts MUST = the total of all creditied accounts for each transaction (debits = credits)
- Accounting equation MUST balance after every transaction

- Always record via business perspective
- Transactions mentions pay = cash 
- Transactions mentions "provides good/service" = record revenue
- Resource used/partially used in the transaction = record expense
  (and decrease asset or increase liability) 
- After record journal entry, need to update each account's balance in the ledger
  (Journal first, then ledger)
** Journalizing & Posting Transactions
- Analyze transactions & source documents
- Apply double-entry accounting
- Record journal entry
- Post entry to ledger
